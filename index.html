01 — Architecture

Android will not let you
close both eyes at once.

So Perde uses two. When a page sets FLAG_SECURE the screenshot comes back black — that is Android's design and no app can undo it without root. But FLAG_SECURE protects the rendered surface. The accessibility tree is a separate structure, and it keeps talking.

Pixels

Channel 2

Text

The accessibility tree is read and scored on evidence, density and counter-evidence.

Unaffected by FLAG_SECURE

Both channels map to the same scale and enter the same decision engine. There is no separate incognito rule and no second set of thresholds — the path that fires in a private tab passes through every false-positive layer that normal browsing does.

The single most discriminating signal is not vocabulary, it is density — matches per 100 words. A news article measures around 2. An encyclopedia entry around 9. A pornographic page measures 23 to 79.

02 — False positives

One frame never blocks.
Not even a certain one.

The fast path exists for unambiguous content, and even that requires two consecutive frames. Six layers sit in front of every block, in order.

  1. 01

    Class weighting

    The model returns five classes. sexy — where beach, sport, fashion and shirtless photos all land — is multiplied by 0.35. drawings is multiplied by zero.

    ×0.35
  2. 02

    Pixel evidence

    Nudity requires skin. The frame is split into a 4×6 grid and the densest tile is measured for skin ratio. If the pixels don't support the model's claim, the score is clipped. Line art — high achromatic ratio, low colourfulness, no skin — is zeroed outright.

    4×6
  3. 03

    Exponential smoothing

    An EMA over the score stops one anomalous frame from moving the decision.

    α 0.45
  4. 04

    Window voting

    Five of the last eight frames must clear the threshold. Scrolling past a thumbnail cannot reach five.

    5 / 8
  5. 05

    Hysteresis

    The block engages at 0.68 and releases at 0.40, after five consecutive clean frames. Without the gap the overlay would flicker on and off at the boundary.

    0.68 → 0.40
  6. 06

    Cooldown

    After a block clears, nothing can re-trigger for four seconds. The overlay itself holds for at least three seconds and lifts unconditionally after eight — a safety valve, because while the curtain is down the screenshot captures the curtain, not the content, so the app can no longer answer whether the screen is clean. Nobody gets locked out of their own phone.

    4 s

Why a stick figure used to trigger it

The classifier cannot separate style from content: it splits line drawings between drawings and hentai, and because hentai carries a weight of 0.95, a crude sketch could clear 0.94 and fire the fast path on a single frame. Two fixes went in — the pixel evidence layer above, and raising the fast path to two consecutive frames. The cost was accepted deliberately and is listed under limits: black-and-white photography and line-art manga now pass.

04 — Sensitivity

The trade-off isn't tunable.
It was measured.

MobileNetV2 puts everything suggestive-but-clothed into one class. A holiday photo and deliberately provocative content produce the same score inside it — the model sees pixels, not intent. So "catch Instagram but never my beach photos" is not achievable with this model. Pick which failure you prefer.

05 — Honest limits

What it doesn't catch.

Measured, accepted, not hidden. If a filter's marketing has no failure list, nobody looked for one.

A page with no text

A single-video page with a euphemistic title and a wordless address, opened in incognito. No pixels, nothing readable.

You normally arrive at such a page from a listing page — and the listing page is blocked. It escapes only if the address is typed directly.

Non-Latin scripts

The dictionary is Latin-script. Cyrillic, Arabic and CJK text is dropped during normalisation.

Spanish, German and French pages are still caught, because the vocabulary overlaps.

Accessibility switched off

The text channel depends entirely on that service. Without it, private tabs return to being a blind spot.

The in-app diagnostics screen says so explicitly rather than failing quietly.

Audio

Video sound is out of scope for both channels. Perde looks and reads; it does not listen.

No plans here — an always-on microphone would contradict the privacy model.

Monochrome imagery

The pixel evidence layer needs skin chroma, so black-and-white photography and line-art manga pass the image channel.

The deliberate price of never blocking a stick figure again.

It is not a cage

Sideloaded means adb uninstall always works from a computer with a cable.

The friction is the 15 minutes, the cable and the effort — not a technical lock. Determination beats it. Autopilot doesn't.

06 — The block screen

Shame is the thing
that keeps the loop running.

The first draft of the block messages was designed to be humiliating. That was the wrong instinct, and the literature is consistent about why: shame produces distress, and distress is relieved by the same behaviour that caused it. The messages became value reminders instead. It is a small difference in wording and a large difference in outcome.

Islam Christianity Judaism Secular Your own words

The scripture lists ship empty on purpose. Original texts are public domain but modern translations are not, and shipping someone else's translation would be a licensing problem. Bring a cleanly licensed source or write your own lines. The secular list is original and complete.

07 — Questions

Straight answers.

What exactly is Perde?

A free, open-source Android app that filters explicit content on your own screen. It runs in the background, reads the screen through two independent channels, and when the evidence and the duration both indicate sustained viewing it covers the screen with a full-screen overlay, shows a message and sends you to the home screen.

The name is Turkish for curtain.

Does it work in incognito?

Yes, through the text channel. Incognito sets FLAG_SECURE, so screen capture returns black frames and the pixels are genuinely gone — no app recovers them without root. But FLAG_SECURE protects the rendered surface, not the accessibility tree. The address bar, page title, headings, link text and image alt text keep arriving, and they go through the same decision engine.

In measurement, an incognito page exposing only its address and title scored 0.965 against a threshold of 0.78. In incognito Perde can't see the pixels. It can still read the content.

Isn't the text channel just a keyword blocklist?

No, and the difference is structural. There is not a single domain name or brand name in the dictionary — domains are infinite and that war is lost on principle. What's in the dictionary is language.

Five things happen to that text. It's normalised, so PORNO, pоrno with a Cyrillic o, p0rn0, p o r n o and Turkish suffixed forms all collapse to one shape. Terms carry four weight classes including a counter class, combined with noisy-OR so no single term can reach 1.0 alone. A corroboration multiplier means one match scores 0.49 while five score 0.96 — one word in a chat cannot block. Density is measured per 100 words. And counter-evidence from banking, health, education and software vocabulary pulls the score down, disabled only once density passes 20 so a page can't immunise itself by printing "secure payment".

Does anything leave my phone?

No. There is no server, no analytics and no network transmission of screen data. The model ships inside the APK. Text from the accessibility tree lives in memory, is overwritten on the next read, and is never written to disk or logged — the diagnostics screen shows counts like s2 m1 d5 g0, never the text itself. With protection off, no reading happens at all.

Worth saying plainly: an accessibility service can read everything on screen. That is a serious permission. You are granting it to a sideloaded app whose source you can read. Think twice before granting it to anything you can't.

Can I switch it off when I get an urge?

Not immediately — turning it off starts a 15-minute delay. Urges peak and fade inside that window, which is the entire design.

And you can always run adb uninstall from a computer. That isn't an oversight. Perde is not a cage; it's friction. Fifteen minutes, a cable, and the effort of using both. Each step is a place to reconsider.

Will it interrupt me during normal use?

On Balanced, effectively never — six layers stand in front of every block and no single frame can cause one. On Strict, beach and holiday photos will trigger. On Rigid, gym and fashion content will too. Those aren't warnings about edge cases, they're measured results, published in the profile table.

Settings, the dialer, contacts, camera, maps, the clock, keyboards and Google Wallet are excluded outright, so a false positive can't land somewhere it would actually hurt. Individual banking apps are not on that list and don't need to be — banking vocabulary is counter-evidence in the text channel, and those apps set FLAG_SECURE anyway.

Which apps does it cover?

Everything except the exclusion list. The default is blacklist mode, not whitelist, because a whitelist is conceptually broken — however long you make it, it stays incomplete, and the app you forgot is exactly the one that gets used.

So a browser nobody has heard of is covered on install, with no list to maintain.

What does it cost me in battery?

Nothing while the screen is off or an excluded app is in front. Frame differencing reduces each frame to a 16×16 fingerprint and skips inference when the screen hasn't changed — roughly 90% of the time in real use, because reading text, a paused video or an idle app all produce identical frames. After 20 calm frames sampling drops from 600 ms to 3 seconds. The model is quantized to about 4 MB and uses the GPU delegate when one is available.

These come from the architecture. They have not been benchmarked on a device, and saying otherwise would be inventing a number.

Why not on the Play Store?

Accessibility service, screen overlay, delayed disabling — the three things that make Perde work are the three Play Store policy restricts to prevent stalkerware. The policy can't distinguish a self-installed filter from a covert monitor, so it treats them the same. Hence a sideloaded APK from GitHub.

Is this spyware?

No. Self-installed, self-configured, reports to nobody. No remote monitoring, no partner dashboard, no logs leaving the device. The permanent status-bar notification is mandatory for a foreground service and there is deliberately no stealth mode — those two properties are what separate a personal filter from stalkerware, and both are visible at all times.

08 — Install

Five permissions,
granted by hand.

There is no runtime prompt for most of these. Android makes you go into Settings on purpose — an app that could grant itself these would be dangerous.

  1. OverlayA button in the app opens the right Settings page.
  2. Usage accessSettings › Special access › Usage access. Manual, no prompt exists.
  3. AccessibilitySettings › Accessibility › Perde. Not optional. It takes screenshots without a prompt and it feeds the text channel. Off means private tabs are a blind spot and protection won't survive a process restart.
  4. Screen captureOnly asked if the accessibility route is unavailable — below API 30. Re-asks on every restart.
  5. Battery unrestrictedSettings › Battery › Unrestricted, or the system kills the service.
